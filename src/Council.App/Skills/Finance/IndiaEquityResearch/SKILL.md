# Indian equity research

Build a sourced company report, not a buy/sell slogan.

1. Identify the correct company, exchange symbol, ISIN and reporting period. Watch for name changes, mergers and multiple share classes.
2. Start with primary evidence: annual reports, quarterly results, investor presentations, exchange filings, credit-rating rationales and regulator disclosures. Use reputable secondary coverage for context, not as a substitute.
3. Review revenue, margins, cash conversion, debt, interest coverage, ROE/ROCE, EPS, dilution, working capital and contingent liabilities across several years.
4. Compare valuation with the company's own history and relevant peers. Explain why sector multiples differ.
5. For Indian companies, inspect promoter holding and pledge, FII/DII/MF ownership changes, related-party transactions, auditor qualifications, corporate actions and material exchange announcements.
6. Apply sector-specific context: NIM/NPA/provision coverage for banks; asset quality and capital adequacy for lenders; order book/execution and working capital for EPC; utilization and spreads for industrials; pipeline and patent exposure for pharma; client concentration and attrition for IT.
7. Present bull/base/bear cases, key catalysts, thesis breakers, data dates and unanswered questions. Distinguish reported facts from interpretation.

Use web search/open for current filings and news. Use `indian_market` only for saved holdings and EOD price checks. Do not claim the current JARVIS tool provides fundamentals, live shareholding or valuation endpoints unless an actual source is connected.
